## 2. Methods for this stage

### Event construction

Events are built by `src/v2_step0.build_events` (8-neighbour, all detections) on
`data/raw/firms` plus `data/raw/firms_v2`, with ignition years 2002-2025 (the function's
`years` parameter extended from the Step 0 default of 2002-2019). The download window is
1 June 2002 to 31 January 2026, so December-ignited events can continue into January.
927,888 detections are used and 324,674 events are kept (Jul-Dec ignition, 2002-2025),
from `results/v2_event_build_summary.csv`.

### Rain

GPCP v2.3 monthly, mean of the three v1 cells per island (`results/gpcp_cells_r3.csv`),
Jul-Dec total in mm (monthly mm/day multiplied by days in the month, then summed). The
algorithm is identical to `src/v2_q3.rain` with `months=[7,8,9,10,11,12]` and `total=True`.

### Step 1 models (2002-2019, per island)

- (a) Proportion of Jul-Dec events that are severe, and very severe, ~ Jul-Dec rain +
  (year - 2002), weighted least squares with weights equal to the number of fire points
  (detections) in all retained events for that island-year (`fire_points` in
  `results/v2_step1_yearly.csv`).
- (b) Seasonal severity skewness ~ Jul-Dec rain + (year - 2002), ordinary least squares.
  Skewness is `scipy.stats.skew(severity, bias=False)` per island-year over the events
  igniting in Jul-Dec.

"% change over 2002-2019" is `100 x 17 x b / fitted value in 2002 at the sample-mean
Jul-Dec rain`, where `b` is the year coefficient. For skewness the same formula is applied
by analogy. Bootstrap 95 percent intervals use the same formula in each resample. 1,000
bootstrap resamples of years, seed 20261102. Drop-2015 and drop-2019 runs are also reported
for the severe model.

### T1 and T2 (2002-2025, per island)

Severe-event proportion ~ Jul-Dec rain + (year - 2002), weighted least squares (same
weights as above). T1 = Sumatra, T2 = Kalimantan. Two-sided p-value of the year
coefficient and its direction are reported. 10,000 bootstrap resamples of years
(seed 20261112) and leave-one-year-out ranges are reported.

### Holm correction

T1-T6 are corrected together. T3-T6 unadjusted p-values are taken from
`results/v2_q3_results.csv` rows with `kind = primary` (not recomputed). T1-T2 p-values
are from `results/v2_t1t2_primary.csv`. Holm-adjusted p-values are in
`results/v2_holm.csv`.

Honest comparison with Sloan et al. 2022:

- The Sloan figures in the table are the ranges across his four regions
  (Indonesia excluding Java and Lesser Sunda; Central-South Sumatra; Southern
  Kalimantan; Targeted Areas). The -16 percent seasonal-severity figure is his
  Sumatra value. Our two island boxes are not the same geographic units as
  Sloan's regions, so this is a side-by-side comparison, not a like-for-like
  reproduction.
- Severe-event decline: Sumatra -11.7 percent and Kalimantan -17.7 percent are
  the same direction as Sloan's 19-27 percent range but smaller in magnitude.
- Very-severe-event decline: Sumatra goes the opposite way, +2.3 percent (Sloan
  23-34 percent decline), with a wide bootstrap interval that includes zero.
  Kalimantan -30.1 percent falls inside Sloan's range.
- Seasonal-severity skewness: Sumatra -48.1 percent (Sloan -16 percent for
  Sumatra) and Kalimantan -30.9 percent are both declines, but both bootstrap
  intervals include zero, so the direction is not robust.

These differences are kept as-is. Beyond the box/region mismatch, our events
may cross the calendar year (Sloan's do not), which can affect the very-severe
share most because very-severe events are rare and sensitive to edge handling.

### Residual diagnostics (descriptive, no test)

Mean WLS residuals from the main T1/T2 model, split at 2016, are in
`results/v2_t1t2_residuals.csv`. This is a descriptive check only; no new test
is run and the main results above are unchanged.

| Test | Period | n | Mean residual (unweighted) | Mean residual (weighted by fire points) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Sumatra | 2002-2015 | 14 | 0.0034 | 0.0051 |
| T1 Sumatra | 2016-2025 | 10 | -0.0652 | -0.0257 |
| T2 Kalimantan | 2002-2015 | 14 | -0.0042 | 0.0021 |
| T2 Kalimantan | 2016-2025 | 10 | -0.0316 | -0.0095 |

For both islands the mean residual is close to zero in 2002-2015 and negative
in 2016-2025, under both the unweighted and fire-point-weighted averages. A
negative residual means the observed severe-event share is below the linear
trend's fitted value, so the post-2016 decline is somewhat steeper than the
straight-line model implies. This is consistent with the post-2016 step model
in the robustness section, but it is described here only as a pattern in the
residuals and is not tested.

## 6. Robustness for T1 and T2

All values from `results/v2_t1t2_robustness.csv`.

- rob2 (2016 step instead of linear year): T1 step coef -0.0715, two-sided p = 5.7e-05;
  T2 step coef -0.0614, two-sided p = 3.9e-04. Both negative and significant.

  [-0.01023, -0.00126]; T2 [-0.00804, -0.00234]. Neither interval includes zero.

The negative year coefficient in T1 and T2 is stable across all four robustness checks.

## 8. Exploratory (no significance claims)

### Skewness over 2002-2025

From `results/v2_exploratory_skewness.csv`. Sumatra skewness ranges from 10.7 (2017) to
125.1 (2006); Kalimantan from 8.4 (2020) to 75.4 (2015). The very large skewness values
reflect the extreme right tail of event severity (a few very large FRP x duration events).
The skewness time series is not tested here.

### 2019 versus 2023

From `results/v2_exploratory_2019_vs_2023.csv`. Both are dry El Nino-type seasons.

| Island | Year | Events | Severe | Very severe | Median severity | FRP sum (MW) | Jul-Dec rain (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sumatra | 2019 | 5935 | 1643 | 833 | 21.9 | 1,230,362 | 865 |
| Sumatra | 2023 | 3955 | 800 | 372 | 17.4 | 274,938 | 1238 |
| Kalimantan | 2019 | 8743 | 2767 | 1309 | 26.4 | 1,261,339 | 1033 |
| Kalimantan | 2023 | 7714 | 1983 | 854 | 23.3 | 529,644 | 1325 |

2019 had more events, more severe and very severe events, higher median severity, and much
higher FRP sum than 2023 on both islands. 2023 was wetter (higher Jul-Dec rain) than 2019
on both islands, consistent with the rain-fire link.

### FRP sum vs rain scatter

`results/v2_exploratory_frp_rain.png` plots Jul-Dec FRP sum against Jul-Dec rain
for each island, 2002-2025, with two smoothers: a quadratic curve (reference)
and a LOWESS curve with `frac = 2/3` from `statsmodels.nonparametric.smoothers_lowess`.
Both smoothers decline as rain increases, so the overall relationship is
negative. The LOWESS curve does not show a sharp step or threshold; it slopes
down gradually over the rain range, with some flattening at the high-rain end.
The quadratic curve is broadly similar in shape but smoother. This is
exploratory; no significance is claimed and no threshold test is performed.

T1/T2 fitted trends are in `results/v2_t1t2_trend.png`.
